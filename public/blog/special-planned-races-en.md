# What Are "Planned Races"? Why They're Great for Beginners [How to Spot a Solid Race]

## Introduction

"Boat racing is hard... isn't there an easier race to predict?"

If that's you, **planned races** are worth knowing about.

A planned race is a race with special rules built into its lineup composition. Because **the conditions are set up to favor lane 1 winning**, these races are much easier for beginners to predict.

This article covers the types of planned races and how to use them.

---

## What is a "planned race"?

### How it works

**A regular race:**
- The lineup (entrants) is determined by lottery or performance ranking
- Lane 1 isn't necessarily favored

**A planned race:**
- The lineup has special rules applied
- Conditions are set up to favor lane 1
- Results tend to be predictable

### Why do planned races exist?

**The purpose:**
1. To give beginner fans races that are easier to predict
2. To boost ticket sales
3. To showcase boat racing's appeal

**The benefit for the venue:**
- Beginners can enjoy the race
- Helps build repeat visitors

---

## Types of planned races

### Type 1: Lane 1 fixed to Class A

**The rule:**
Lane 1 is always assigned a Class A racer (A1 or A2).

**Character:**
- The most common type of planned race
- Lane 1's win rate runs higher than normal
- Held at nearly every venue nationwide

**Examples:**
- A "fixed approach" race 1, etc.
- Only lane 1 is Class A, lanes 2–6 are Class B

### Type 2: Seeded lineup

**The rule:**
Lane 1 goes to the top performer of that race meet so far.

**Character:**
- Lane 1 goes to a racer in good form
- Their motor is also often performing well
- Tends to produce a very predictable result

**Examples:**
- A featured race in the back half of qualifying
- Lane 1 goes to the top points-average racer

### Type 3: Fixed-approach race

**The rule:**
Racers are required to approach in lane order (no jockeying for position).

**Character:**
- No pre-race jockeying for lane position
- The lane-1 racer is guaranteed lane 1
- Easier to predict

**The benefit:**
No need to predict lane approach — the race comes down purely to racer and motor ability.

### Type 4: Lane 1 Class A1 + outside lanes Class B

**The rule:**
Lane 1 is Class A1, while lanes 5–6 are Class B.

**Character:**
- A clear gap in ability
- Lane 1 has an overwhelming advantage
- Lane 1's win rate can exceed 70%

**Examples:**
- Races named "First Battle," "Early Bird Race," etc.

## When planned races are held

### By time of day

**Early morning (races 1–2):**
- The most common time for planned races
- Set up to be predictable for beginners

**Midday (races 5–8):**
- Fewer planned races
- Usually a standard lineup

**Final races (11–12):**
- Semi-main and main races
- Sometimes a seeded lineup

### By day of the race meet

**Day 1:**
- More planned races
- Aimed at drawing in fans

**Mid-meet (days 3–4):**
- Seeded lineups based on results so far
- Lane 1 goes to a racer in good form

**Final day:**
- Semifinal and final races
- Not "planned" per se, but lane 1 is still favored

## How to spot a planned race

### Method 1: Check the "notes" column in the race program

The race program will note things like "fixed approach" or "Class A seeded."

### Method 2: Check the race name

Planned races often get a distinctive name.

**Common race names:**
- "First Battle"
- "Morning Special"
- "Sunrise Race"
- "This Is the One!"
- "Break Time"

### Method 3: Check races 1 and 2

**Early morning tends to have more planned races:**
- Race 1: fixed approach, lane 1 Class A
- Race 2: a similar setup

Start by checking the lineup for races 1 and 2.

### Method 4: The venue's official site

Each boat racing venue's official site publishes information about its planned races.

**What you can find:**
- The planned race's name
- Rule details
- Which race numbers it applies to

## How to bet on a planned race

### Approach 1: Anchor around lane 1

**The basic strategy:**
Build your bet around lane 1 finishing 1st.

**Example bets:**
- Win: lane 1
- Place: lane 1
- Exacta: 1-all
- Trifecta: 1-all-all

### Approach 2: Play it safe with Place

**The idea:**
Even if lane 1 doesn't win, it tends to still finish top 2 in a planned race.

**The upside:**
- High hit rate
- Less risk of a big loss
- Sustainable for beginners

**The downside:**
- Lower payouts (roughly ¥110–150)

### Approach 3: Focus your bet on 2nd place and below

**The idea:**
Assume lane 1 takes 1st, and focus your prediction on 2nd and 3rd.

**What to target:**
- A Class A racer in lane 2 or 3
- A racer with a strong motor
- A racer with a good exhibition time

**Example bets:**
- Trifecta: 1-2-3, 1-2-4, 1-3-2, 1-3-4
- Trio: 1-2-3, 1-2-4, 1-3-4

---

## Things to watch out for with planned races

### Caution 1: Low payouts

**Betting concentrates on the favorite:**
Since planned races are known to be predictable, betting money concentrates on lane 1.

**The result:**
- Win odds around 1.2–1.5x
- Risk of a break-even hit (winning but still losing money)

**How to handle it:**
- Avoid Win and Place bets
- Target Trio/Trifecta with a narrowed field to get a real payout

### Caution 2: It's not a guarantee

**Even in a planned race, lane 1 can still lose:**
- If the motor is underperforming
- If the racer is out of form
- Due to an accident (capsize or falling overboard)

**Don't over-trust it:**
Don't assume "it's a planned race, so it's guaranteed to hit."

### Caution 3: Not every race in the day is planned

**An easy mistake:**
Race 1 might be a planned race, while race 3 onward reverts to a standard lineup.

**How to check:**
Always check the lineup for each individual race.

- Motor 2nd-place-or-better rate
- Exhibition time

## Summary

**Key points on planned races:**

1. **Conditions are set up to favor lane 1**: fixed Class A, seeded lineups, and more
2. **Common in the early morning (races 1–2)**: check the early races first
3. **Great for beginners**: easier to predict, higher hit rate
4. **Lower payouts**: watch for break-even hits, target Trio/Trifecta for a real return

Planned races are the perfect entry point for a boat racing beginner to experience the joy of actually hitting a pick.

Start by learning the basics of ticket buying through planned races, and gradually work your way up to harder races.
