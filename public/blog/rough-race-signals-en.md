## What exactly is a "volatile race"?

Let's define this clearly first. In boat racing, a "volatile race" refers to a result like the following:

- **Lane 1 doesn't finish in the top 3**
- **A boat from lanes 4–6 wins**
- **The Trifecta pays out ¥10,000 or more**

In other words, "a race that doesn't go by standard theory and produces a large payout."

BoatAI's analysis shows that about 15–20% of all races qualify as "volatile races." Spotting that 15–20% in advance lets you optimize your betting strategy.

## Signal 1: Lane 1's national win rate is under 5.5

Lane 1 has the biggest advantage in boat racing. But when **the lane-1 racer's overall ability is weak**, that advantage erodes significantly.

### What the AI found

- In races where lane 1's national win rate is **under 5.5**, lane 1's win rate drops to **around 30%**
- The 1st-place rate for lanes 4–6 rises to **more than double** the normal level

### What to look for

Check the lane-1 racer's "national win rate" in the race program.

- **6.0+**: Strong ability, lane 1 is favored
- **5.5–6.0**: Somewhat uncertain
- **Under 5.5**: High chance of a volatile race ⚠️

You can also check each racer's win-rate data on BoatAI's prediction screen.

## Signal 2: A 20+ point gap in motor 2nd-place-or-better rate

Motor performance matters even more than racer ability. In particular, when "lane 1's motor is weak but an outside lane's motor is strong," a big upset becomes much more likely.

### What the AI found

- When the gap between lane 1's and lanes 4–6's motor 2nd-place-or-better rate is **20 points or more**, the odds of a volatile race are **2.3x higher**
- Be especially cautious with an extreme gap like "lane 1 in the 20% range, lane 5 in the 40% range"

### What to look for

Check the "motor 2nd-place-or-better rate" in the race program.

**Example:**
- Lane 1: motor 2nd-place rate 28%
- Lane 5: motor 2nd-place rate 52%

→ A 24-point gap, signaling a high chance of a volatile race

BoatAI automatically calculates this gap and factors it into the "Upset Index."

## Signal 3: A headwind of 5m or more

Weather conditions, especially **wind**, have a big impact on how a race unfolds.

### What the AI found

- In races with a headwind of 5m or more, lane 1's win rate drops by **about 10%**
- Starts become harder, opening up chances for outside-lane racers

### What to look for

Check the pre-race weather report.

- **Tailwind**: favors lane 1 (easier to build speed)
- **Headwind up to 3m**: barely any effect
- **Headwind 5m+**: high chance of a volatile race ⚠️

Be especially cautious on a day when wind direction shifts suddenly.

---

## Signal 4: Lane 1 looks close to a flying start in the start exhibition

The start exhibition is an important indicator of how a racer's start timing will feel in the actual race.

### What the AI found

- A racer who starts close to a flying start (too early) in the exhibition tends to **overcorrect and start late** in the actual race
- Races where lane 1 looked close to a flying start in the exhibition were **1.8x more likely** to turn volatile

### What to look for

Check the start timing in the exhibition run.

- **0.10–0.15 in the start exhibition**: ideal
- **0.05 or below**: close to a flying start, may overcorrect in the real race
- **0.20 or above**: a slow start, cause for concern

BoatAI factors exhibition-time data into its analysis as well.

---

## Signal 5: Lane 1's average start timing (ST) is 0.17 or worse

Each racer's historical **average start timing (ST)** is another important indicator.

### What the AI found

- In races where lane 1's average ST is **0.17 or worse**, the chance of a slow start rises significantly
- Racers with an average ST above 0.20 see their start success rate drop by **about 20%**

### What to look for

Check "average ST" in the race program.

- **0.15 or better**: a strong starter, favors lane 1
- **0.16–0.17**: standard
- **0.18 or worse**: cause for concern, higher chance of volatility ⚠️

## When multiple signals stack up, confidence rises

What matters most is **whether multiple signals show up at the same time**.

### Approximate probability of a volatile race

- **1 signal**: ~20% chance of volatility
- **2 signals**: ~35% chance of volatility
- **3+ signals**: ~50%+ chance of volatility ⚠️⚠️

For example, a situation like this:

1. Lane 1's win rate is 5.2 (Signal 1)
2. Lane 1's motor 2nd-place rate is 25%, lane 5's is 45% (Signal 2)
3. A headwind of 6m (Signal 3)

→ **3 signals stack up, and the race is very likely to be volatile**

In a situation like this, using BoatAI's Upset Index to guide a longshot bet for a bigger payout is an effective approach.
