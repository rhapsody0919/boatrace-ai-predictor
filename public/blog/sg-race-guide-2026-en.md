# The Complete 2026 SG Race Guide — Each Race's Character and Betting Strategy

"I want to hit my picks in SG races, but what should I actually base my predictions on?"

SG races, the pinnacle of boat racing, are held only 8 times a year. The nation's top Class A1 racers gather for these events, where the competition looks nothing like a regular race.

This article walks through the 2026 SG race calendar and breaks down each race's character and betting strategy.

---

## What is an SG race? — The 8 biggest races in boat racing

SG stands for "Special Grade," the **highest grade** in boat racing.

### SG race basics

- **Races per year:** 8
- **Eligibility:** Top racers, mostly Class A1
- **Winner's prize:** ¥30 million to over ¥100 million
- **Duration:** 6 days per race (qualifying → semifinal → final)

Only a handful of the roughly 1,600 licensed boat racers nationwide ever qualify for an SG race. That's exactly why the competition level — and the difficulty of predicting it — is so high.

## Each SG race's character and how to bet on it

### 1. Boat Race Classic (March)

The first SG of the year, featuring racers who performed well the previous season. **Early-year form tends to carry straight through.**

**Strategy:**
- Watch racers who were hot in the back half of the previous year
- At venues with new motors, spotting the "lucky" motor matters
- Focus heavily on exhibition times in days 1–2

### 2. Boat Race All-Star (May)

Since entrants are chosen by fan vote, this race has **a festival-like atmosphere with popular racers front and center**. The Dream Race lineup is filled with the top vote-getters.

**Strategy:**
- Odds tend to skew toward popular racers, opening up longshot opportunities
- In the Dream Race, popular racers face off against each other and odds tend to split
- Remember: "popularity" and "ability" don't always align

### 3. Grand Champion (June)

Entrants are mainly selected from SG/G1 winners — **a field of genuinely elite racers**.

**Strategy:**
- With such a high overall caliber of racer, the ability gap between competitors is the smallest of any SG
- Inside wire-to-wire win rate tends to be lower than in regular races
- Races often come down to motor performance, making exhibition data analysis key

### 4. Ocean Cup (July)

Held in summer, when **temperature and humidity significantly affect motor performance**.

**Strategy:**
- Rising temperatures tend to reduce motor output, making overtakes harder to land
- Inside-pass and overtake-and-pass patterns become more common
- Local racers tend to know how to handle the heat — worth checking

### 5. Boat Race Memorial (August)

**A battle of local representatives**, with each venue nominating its own entrant. Motivation runs high as racers compete for their home venue's pride.

**Strategy:**
- Locally nominated racers know the water intimately and are often skilled at turns
- The "local racer × home venue" fit is an especially important data point
- Since each venue's ace-level racer is present, the semifinals onward can be more predictable

### 6. Boat Race Derby (October)

Positioned as **the year's ultimate showdown among top win-rate racers**. Autumn conditions tend to stabilize motor performance, producing results that track closer to raw ability.

**Strategy:**
- With top annual win-rate racers gathered, the inside lane's reliability is relatively high
- Motors stabilize in autumn, favoring a speed-focused race
- Qualifying-round points races get fierce — don't overlook a racer pushing hard to secure a spot

### 7. Challenge Cup (November)

Featuring top prize-money earners, this race functions as **a warm-up with Grand Prix qualification on the line**.

**Strategy:**
- Racers near the Grand Prix qualification cutoff tend to race aggressively
- Racers who've already secured their Grand Prix spot may use this race to test motor adjustments instead
- Reading each racer's "stakes" in the race is key to predicting it

### 8. Grand Prix / Grand Prix Series (December)

Featuring the year's top 18 prize-money earners, this is **the pinnacle showdown of the boat racing world**. The winner's prize exceeds ¥100 million, and it's the year's grand finale.

**Strategy:**
- The trial (qualifying) round splits racers into 2 groups, with the top 3 from each advancing to the final
- The final is a single winner-take-all race among 6 racers, where the lane draw can decide everything
- The Grand Prix Series (for racers ranked 19th and below) runs alongside it and is comparatively easier to predict

## 3 things to watch when predicting SG races

### 1. Everyone is Class A1 — the "skill gap" nearly disappears

In regular races, a Class A1 racer in lane 1 gives you a strong expectation of a wire-to-wire win. In SG races, **everyone is Class A1**, and lane 1's wire-to-wire rate tends to run 5–10% lower than in regular races.

**Motor performance and same-day start power** matter more than raw lane advantage.

### 2. The motor lottery has a big impact

SG races use motors assigned to the host venue via a lottery. Racers have to adapt to unfamiliar motors in a short time, which favors **racers skilled at tuning**.

Data worth checking:
- Motor 2nd- and 3rd-place rates
- Exhibition time and straightaway time
- Time changes from the previous day (a sign of tuning effectiveness)

### 3. Assess the home-venue advantage

Local racers, or racers who specialize in the host venue's water conditions, gain **a real advantage in course positioning and turns**. This local edge is especially significant in a locally-nominated race like the Memorial.
