## 6 key factors in a good prediction

### 1. Racer win rate (the most important factor)

**Win rate** is the single most reliable measure of a racer's ability.

**Rough benchmarks:**
- **7.00+**: Top-tier racer (SG class)
- **6.00–6.99**: Strong Class A1 racer
- **5.00–5.99**: Mid-tier
- **4.99 and below**: Worth extra caution

✅ **Tip:** If a Class A1 racer (win rate 6.00+) is in lane 1, making them your core pick is the standard rule of thumb. Ryujin Radar's analysis shows that **a Class A1 racer in lane 1 finishes 1st over 60% of the time**.

That said, judging by overall win rate alone is risky. Also check the racer's **venue-specific win rate**, since water conditions vary significantly by venue — a racer with a strong national win rate can still struggle at a particular venue.

---

### 2. Motor 2nd-place-or-better rate

In boat racing, racers draw motors by lottery, and **the difference in motor performance** has a major effect on race outcomes.

| Motor 2nd-place-or-better rate | Rating | Impact on prediction |
|---------------|------|------------|
| **45%+** | Elite motor | Can be trusted as your core pick |
| **40–44%** | Strong motor | High reliability |
| **35–39%** | Standard | Weigh alongside other factors |
| **30–34%** | Slightly underperforming | Discount somewhat |
| **Under 30%** | Poor performance | Be cautious even with a strong racer |

✅ **Tip:** Judge by the **combination** of racer ability × motor performance. A Class A1 racer paired with a strong motor (40%+) is the most reliable pattern.

---

### 3. Lane-by-lane performance

Boat racing's defining feature is **the overwhelming advantage of the inside lane**. But how much of an advantage each lane has varies significantly by venue.

**National average 1st-place rate by lane:**

| Lane | 1st-place rate | Character |
|-------|-------|------|
| Lane 1 | ~55% | Overwhelmingly favored |
| Lane 2 | ~14% | Wins mainly by inside pass |
| Lane 3 | ~12% | Wins by overtake or overtake-and-pass |
| Lane 4 | ~11% | Occasional single big overtake |
| Lane 5 | ~6% | Depends heavily on how the race develops |
| Lane 6 | ~2% | A tough position |

✅ **Tip:** Check a racer's track record by lane. A racer known for a strong overtake-and-pass from lane 3, for example, deserves a bump when they draw lane 3.

---

### 4. Start timing (ST)

Boat racing uses a **flying start** format — the ideal is to cross the start line exactly as the countdown clock hits zero.

**How to read average ST:**
- **0.10–0.13**: Standard
- **Under 0.10**: A start specialist (a real edge)
- **0.15+**: A weakness in start timing

⚠️ **Note:** Even a racer with a good average ST may start more cautiously if they're carrying a flying-start (F) penalty. Keep in mind that **a racer with an F penalty carries some risk of a slow start**.

✅ **Tip:** If the inside-lane racer has slow ST and an outside-lane racer is a start specialist, expect **a race where an overtake is more likely to succeed**.

---

### 5. Exhibition run

The exhibition run is the "final piece of evidence" before the actual race.

**3 things to check:**

**① Exhibition time**
- A time near the top of the field suggests a strong motor
- Be cautious, though — some racers don't run at full throttle in the exhibition

**② Start exhibition**
- A practice run close to the actual start
- A racer who falls noticeably behind here is a red flag for the real race

**③ Turn quality**
- A smooth turn signals good form
- Swinging wide or wobbling suggests the racer isn't fully tuned in

✅ **Tip:** Even if a racer's stats look good, **downgrade your evaluation a notch** if their exhibition run looks weak. Conversely, an unremarkable racer with a standout exhibition run can be a longshot worth considering.

---

### 6. Weather and water conditions

Boat racing takes place outdoors, so weather has a strong influence.

**Wind direction and its effect on predictions:**

| Condition | Effect on inside lane | Prediction tip |
|------|-----------------|-------------|
| Tailwind 1–2m | Slight advantage | Standard predictions apply |
| Tailwind 3m+ | Starting gets harder | Watch for overtakes |
| Headwind 1–2m | Advantage | Trust the inside lane |
| Headwind 3m+ | Inside passes land more easily | Watch lane 2 |
| Crosswind | Harder to control the boat | Weight racer skill more heavily |

⚠️ **Wind of 5m or more raises the chance of a volatile race.** Use Ryujin Radar's Upset Index too, to decide between a conservative or aggressive bet structure.

## 5 common mistakes beginners make

### ❌ Mistake 1: Betting on odds alone

"It's popular, so it'll probably hit" is a risky assumption. Odds are just **an aggregate of everyone else's predictions**, and they aren't always right. Get in the habit of checking the data yourself.

### ❌ Mistake 2: Trusting lane 1 unconditionally

Lane 1's national average 1st-place rate is around 55% — which also means **it misses about 45% of the time**. Always check the racer's ability, motor, and ST before trusting it.

### ❌ Mistake 3: Spreading your bets too wide

Adding more combinations to raise your hit rate defeats the purpose if it turns into a break-even (or losing) bet even when you win. **5–8 combinations is the basic rule for Trifecta bets.**

### ❌ Mistake 4: Skipping the exhibition run

Many bettors finalize their prediction from the entry list alone, but the exhibition run reveals **real, same-day condition**. Especially after a motor adjustment, the exhibition run is the only place that change shows up.

### ❌ Mistake 5: Chasing your losses

Betting big right after a loss is one of the worst things you can do. **Set a daily budget and stay level-headed about which races to bet on** — that's what actually improves your long-term return rate.
